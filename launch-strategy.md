## 1. The Geofenced Launch Playbook

### Pre-Launch: Selecting Your Beachhead

**Criteria for the first community:**
- Dense social graph (everyone knows each other)
- High communication frequency (daily in-person contact)
- Early in their cycle (school semester, work quarter) so there's energy and novelty-seeking
- Ideally ahead of other similar communities (e.g., Georgia schools start earlier than most US schools)

**Why schools work:** Students see each other every day. Gossip travels in hours. A new app
is social currency. One connected student can trigger a cascade across an entire grade in
a single day.

**The 40% Rule:** Target 40% penetration of your beachhead community within 24 hours. If you
can't hit this, the product or distribution isn't working. Kill it or iterate.

### Launch Day Execution

**The tbh Playbook (step by step):**

1. Create community-specific Instagram accounts (e.g., "@tbh_[schoolname]")
2. Set accounts to PRIVATE
3. Follow every student who has the school tagged in their profile
4. Post a mysterious bio: "You're invited to tbh at [School Name]. Stay tuned."
5. Accept NO follow requests — let curiosity build for 1-3 days
6. At the calculated moment (Bier used ~4 PM, right after school dismissal):
   - Switch ALL accounts from private to public simultaneously
   - Update bios with the download link
   - Accept ALL pending follow requests at once
7. This creates a synchronized explosion — hundreds of students get notifications at the same time
8. The app appears in "everyone's" feed simultaneously, creating social proof cascade

**Why this works:**
- Scarcity (private account) creates desire
- Synchronized reveal creates a shared social moment
- Peer pressure kicks in: "everyone's downloading this"
- The 4 PM timing catches students when they're checking phones and talking to friends

### Post-Launch Monitoring

- Watch App Store rank as the primary scoreboard
- If rank drops, kill or reimagine features overnight
- Rewrite copy and tune notifications before buying installs (never buy installs)
- Track penetration percentage per community daily

## 2. Social Channel Tactics

### Organic Social Engineering

**The Burner Account Strategy:**
- Create accounts that appear teen-run, not corporate
- Multiple accounts per school/community
- Vary content style across accounts (some meme-focused, some "insider info")
- Cross-promote between accounts to create the illusion of widespread organic buzz

**The DM Campaign:**
- Identify the most connected students (high follower counts, frequent engagement)
- Send personalised DMs with a download link
- Frame it as exclusive: "You're one of the first people at [school] to try this"
- Connected students become unpaid evangelists if the product delivers

### Platform-Specific Tactics

**Instagram:**
- Stories with polls/questions about the app (interactive, visible)
- Countdown stickers to launch moment
- "Close Friends" lists for exclusive beta access

**TikTok:**
- Create content that demonstrates the app's value in action
- Use trending sounds and formats
- Show authentic reactions to receiving compliments/content
- 50+ videos per day across multiple creator accounts (Dupe.com approach)

**Snapchat:**
- Share previews of in-app content via Snaps
- Use Snap streaks as a parallel engagement mechanic
- (Note: Bier had his app removed from SnapKit — be cautious about dependency on any single platform)

**X/Twitter:**
- Build founder personality/brand (Bier himself does this masterfully)
- Create controversy or narrative (Explode's "spite app" launch is a textbook example)
- Engage directly with users and critics

## 3. Content-First Distribution

### The 50-Video-a-Day Model (Dupe.com)

**Production approach:**
- Small team or solo creator making short-form video at massive volume
- Each video is a self-contained demonstration of value
- Multiple accounts to create omnipresence effect
- Result: $100K MRR in 60 days

**Content types that convert:**
- "Watch me use this app" (screen recordings with reaction)
- "I can't believe this is free" (value surprise)
- "My friends' reactions to..." (social proof)
- Before/after comparisons
- "POV: you just discovered [app]"

### The "Seem Everywhere" Principle

Marketing message frequency rule: a potential user needs ~3 exposures before they'll download.
Saturating a small geographic/social area with content is more effective than a thin spread
across a large audience. The goal: every potential user should feel like "everyone is talking
about this app."

## 4. Scaling Beyond the First Community

### The Expansion Playbook

```
Stage 1: Dominate one community (40% penetration)
Stage 2: Let demand bleed into adjacent communities
  - Users in community A tell friends in community B
  - Create FOMO in community B: "Why don't we have this yet?"
Stage 3: Open to adjacent community when demand is visible
Stage 4: Repeat, expanding the radius each time
Stage 5: Go state by state, not national
```

### Controlling the Rollout

- Cap new geographic areas until server stability is confirmed
- The cap itself creates exclusivity (waitlist effect)
- Users on the waitlist become evangelists: they tell friends, building demand
- When you open a new area, the pent-up demand creates another launch-day effect

### Avoiding Premature Scaling

If you go national before achieving density in local communities:
- The experience is empty (no friends on the app)
- The cold start problem kills engagement
- You burn through potential users who try it once, find it empty, and never return
- These are "burned" users — extremely hard to re-acquire

## 5. When to Sell vs. Scale

### Bier's Exit Strategy

Bier's pattern: optimise for rapid acquisition and lucrative exits, not for building the
next dominant social network.

**tbh:** Sold to Facebook for ~$30M, nine weeks after launch, while still at peak charts.
**Gas:** Sold to Discord within six months of debut.

### The Logic

- Consumer social apps with a single novel mechanic often hit a retention cliff
- Building a durable communication product is a "black swan event" (once a decade)
- Viral growth and sustainable engagement are different challenges
- Selling at peak maximises value; waiting risks the post-hype plateau
- Big companies take 12-24 months to respond to competitive threats — sell before they build it

### The Counter-Argument (acknowledged)

Both tbh and Gas shut down after acquisition. The experiment-driven culture that birthed
them didn't survive inside large organisations. If your goal is lasting impact rather than
a financial exit, the Bier playbook needs adaptation for the retention/community phase.

## 6. The Death Clock Play — [Utility] launches without a social graph

Health apps are single-player utilities that resonate most with older audiences: structurally the
worst possible growth profile, and exactly the case where everything in §1 is useless. Bier advised
two changes and drove **CAC down to pennies**.

**1. Rename for word-of-mouth.** "Most Days" → **"Death Clock."** The name itself became the hook. It
drove word-of-mouth *and* got picked up by mainstream national press (including a mention on *The
Late Show with Stephen Colbert*) — coverage a sleepy name would never have earned.

**2. Manufacture a shareable artefact.** They added a survey that predicted your death date, plus a
projection of what you'd look like as you age. This did two jobs at once: it made an abstract value
proposition (habits affect longevity) **viscerally tangible**, and it generated **personalised
content users wanted to show other people**.

**Result:** No. 6 in the iOS Health charts.

**The generalisable lesson for [Utility] apps:** you don't need a social graph. You need (a) a name
people repeat, and (b) an output artefact worth showing someone. If your utility produces no
artefact, invent one — that's a product decision, not a marketing one.

## 7. Crisis Planning — the Gas Hoax

At peak growth, a viral misinformation campaign claimed Gas was involved in human trafficking.
**3% of users deleted their accounts every single day.**

The counter-offensive had three parts:

1. Secure press headlines explicitly debunking the claim
2. Call police chiefs directly to get official statements
3. **Embed a debunking video inside the account-deletion screen** — intercepting churn at the exact
   moment of decision, not in a blog post nobody reads

**Deletions fell to 0.1%/day.**

**[Universal]** If you build for teens, assume a negative narrative is coming. Two rules: your
rebuttal has to be more viral than the accusation, and it has to be placed at the point of churn.
Write the crisis plan before you need it.
